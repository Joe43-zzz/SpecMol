\title{[Experiment, Analysis, and Benchmark]\\
A Random-Pair Null Protocol for Auditing Frozen 3D-Geometry
Injection in Molecular Property Prediction}

% alt A (boundary-question framing):
% \title{[Experiment, Analysis, and Benchmark] When Does Frozen 3D-Pair
% Injection Help a Molecular Spectral GNN? A Matched-Protocol Audit}

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
%  ABSTRACT (draft, ~210 words)
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Pretrained 3D molecular encoders are increasingly injected into 2D
property-prediction models as frozen geometric features, yet whether the
\emph{real} geometry---rather than the added parameters---drives any gain is
rarely tested. We present a matched-protocol audit of frozen 3D-pair injection
(Uni-Mol pair representations) into a Chebyshev spectral GNN, built around three
reusable controls: (i) a shape-matched \textbf{random-pair null} that replaces
the pretrained pair tensor with Gaussian noise at the consumption site, isolating
geometry from architecture; (ii) a \textbf{frozen-probe$\rightarrow$finetune
absolute correction} that prevents an under-trained linear probe from
mis-ranking encoders; and (iii) a \textbf{matched-split, descriptor-RF ceiling}
that quantifies fingerprint saturation. Across the entire geometry-signal
spectrum---saturated ADMET classification, composition-dominated QM7 atomization
energy, and the geometry-pure QM9 dipole moment evaluated with \emph{true
B3LYP/6-31G(2df,p) DFT coordinates}---every deep 3D-injection variant ties its
own random-pair null and a 2D baseline, and a charge-aware random forest matches
or beats all of them (e.g.\ on DFT-coordinate QM9 dipole, RF $0.845$ vs.\ best
deep $0.880$ RMSE). A gate-movement probe rules out a wiring artifact: enabling
the richest co-update mechanism makes it \emph{worse}, not better. We release a
fully regenerable evaluation harness. The contribution is a transferable
falsification protocol and a boundary map, not a new predictor.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(C1) A random-pair null protocol for 3D-injection claims, under a
matched inductive split.} We argue any ``geometry helps'' claim must clear a
shape-matched random-pair null---replacing the frozen 3D pair tensor with
\texttt{randn\_like} noise at the exact consumption site, holding architecture,
split, seeds, and budget fixed. We implement it for three injection mechanisms
(static edge gate, pair-biased attention, atom$\leftrightarrow$pair co-update)
and show the real pair never separates from its null within seed variance.

\item \textbf{(C2) A frozen-probe$\rightarrow$finetune absolute correction that
overturns a concrete conclusion.} A frozen linear probe ranks encoders
unreliably; we report the absolute delta on one pipeline (e.g.\ BACE
$0.822\!\rightarrow\!0.886$ AUC) and show it flips an apparent ``deep is weak''
reading. Properly finetuned, the 2D baseline ties a descriptor RF on BACE and
beats it on BBBP/FreeSolv---yet 3D injection still adds nothing.

\item \textbf{(C3) A geometry-signal boundary map, including a true-DFT stress
test.} We trace where injection is inert along a geometry axis: saturated ADMET
$\rightarrow$ composition-dominated QM7 $\rightarrow$ geometry-pure QM9 dipole
with \emph{true DFT coordinates}. The descriptor ceiling holds throughout; a
gate-movement probe shows the negative is data-bound, not a design/wiring
artifact (\S\ref{sec:dft}).

\item \textbf{(C4) A regenerable evaluation harness.} Every table cell
regenerates from released per-seed JSON via a single script; null-protocol,
split-reconstruction, and matched baselines are released as first-class
artifacts.
\end{itemize}

\section{The geometry-pure stress test: true DFT coordinates}
\label{sec:dft}

The strongest objection to a ``3D does not help'' result is that our targets are
not geometry-driven, or that our 3D source is too coarse. We close both by
running the cleanest possible positive control: the QM9 \emph{dipole moment}
$\mu$---a property that is, by construction, a function of the 3D charge
distribution---fed Uni-Mol pair representations extracted from the molecules'
\emph{true} GDB-9 DFT-optimized geometries (B3LYP/6-31G(2df,p)), not RDKit/MMFF
re-embeddings. If frozen 3D injection ever helps, it should help here.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We hold the 2D graph featurization \emph{identical} to the matched cell and
change only the coordinates Uni-Mol sees: true DFT coordinates are transferred
onto the canonical atom order by validated subgraph isomorphism (a per-molecule
bond-length gate flags any misalignment; $0/{\sim}2.75\!\times\!10^{5}$ bonds
violated). We conservatively drop the ${\sim}16\%$ of molecules whose published
DFT-SDF connectivity disagrees with the SMILES, leaving $12{,}530$ molecules
($1{,}256$ test). We finetune end-to-end the same seven arms
(V0; static gate V2-T5; pair-biased attention T7; co-update T8; and the
random-pair null of each), $n{=}3$ seeds, and compute the matched
charge-aware RF on the \emph{same} molecule subset.

\paragraph{Result: the ceiling holds even here (Table~\ref{tab:dft}).}
Real DFT geometry does not change the verdict. The static gate and attention read
no geometry ($\mu$ effect $+0.0003$ and $+0.017$, i.e.\ the real pair is no
better---or worse---than its shuffled null); the richest co-update shows a small
but shrinking effect ($-0.010$, vs.\ $-0.033$ under MMFF). Crucially, the best
deep arm ($0.880$ RMSE) is \emph{beaten} by a charge-aware descriptor RF on the
identical subset ($0.845$). The descriptor ceiling that bounds deep 3D-injection
on saturated ADMET and composition-dominated QM7 still bounds it on the
geometry-pure dipole with true DFT geometry.

\paragraph{Mechanism completeness: closing the wiring loophole.}
One could object that the co-update's near-identity (ReZero) gates never train,
so geometry was never given a fair channel. We test this directly. The dedicated
high-learning-rate finetune group originally matched only the attention gate,
silently leaving the co-update's node/pair/FFN gates at the slow encoder rate; we
fix the group and re-run on $\mu$. The gates now \emph{move} (pair gate to
${\approx}0.3$), confirming the channel is active---yet the co-update gets
\emph{worse} ($0.989$ vs.\ $0.966$ RMSE) and its apparent geometry effect
collapses ($-0.033\!\rightarrow\!-0.003$). Per our pre-registered rule (gates
move \emph{and} error improves $\Rightarrow$ a suppressed signal; gates move
without improvement $\Rightarrow$ data-bound), the negative is \emph{data-bound,
not a design or wiring artifact}: there is no geometry signal being withheld,
and the small MMFF ``effect'' was an edge-weight side path, not the co-update.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
Across saturated ADMET, composition-dominated QM7, and the geometry-pure QM9
dipole with \emph{true DFT coordinates}, frozen 3D-pair injection is inert: it
ties its own random-pair null and a 2D baseline, and a cheap charge-aware
descriptor RF matches or beats it everywhere. This is a clean, mechanism-complete
boundary, obtained with the protocol of C1--C2 and released in full.
